Account Violations (Hard Breach)Copy link
Max Daily Drawdown3%Max Total Drawdown6% (Intraday Trailing)Hard breach are serious violations to our established rules. A hard breach happens when a trader exceeds the maximum daily loss or total drawdown limit, resulting in the immediate closure of the account.
1. Max Daily Drawdown – 3% Copy link
What does this mean?Copy link
The maximum daily loss limit is 3% of your daily starting balance.
How is it calculated?Copy link
The daily drawdown is reset every day at 5 PM EST. At the reset, we take the higher of your balance and equity and set your daily limit 3% below it. The limit stays the same until the next reset.
Worked exampleFor a $100,000 account, the daily drawdown limit on the first day is $3,000, so your balance or equity must stay above $97,000. If your account starts the next day at $104,000, your daily limit for that day is $100,880 (3% below $104,000).
2. Max Total Drawdown – 6% (Intraday Trailing)Copy link
What does this mean?Copy link
The maximum total drawdown of Instant Premium accounts is a fixed amount equal to 6% of your initial balance. On a $100,000 account that is $6,000, so your limit starts at $94,000. When your balance or equity reaches a new high, the limit moves up so it stays $6,000 below that high. This happens during the trading day, as soon as the new high is reached. The limit never moves down, and it stops moving once it reaches your initial balance.
Worked exampleFor a $100,000 account:If your account reaches $104,000, your trailing drawdown limit becomes $98,000 ($6,000 below your highest equity).If your account then drops to $103,000, your trailing drawdown limit stays at $98,000.If your account later reaches $107,000, the limit would be $101,000, but it stops at $100,000, your initial balance, and stays there.
N.B - The Maximum Loss Limit resets after each payout.
Once your payout is approved, your balance goes back to your initial balance and your limit is set again, $6,000 below it. From there it trails up the same way and stops at your initial balance again. Your daily drawdown limit is also set again from the new balance.
Worked exampleFor a $100,000 account:Your account is at $106,000 and your limit has reached $100,000.You request a payout. Once it is approved, your balance is back to $100,000 and your limit resets to $94,000, so you have $6,000 of room again.Your daily limit for the rest of that day is $97,000 (3% below $100,000).If your account then reaches $102,000, your limit becomes $96,000. Once your account reaches $106,000, it stops at $100,000 again.
Understanding the 1.5% Max Floating Loss RuleCopy link
If your floating (unrealized) loss reaches -1.5% of your account balance at any moment, the account will be permanently closed. This rule enforces strict risk management and prevents excessive losses.For example, on a $50,000 account, if your combined open trade losses reach -$750 (-1.5%), the account will be immediately closed.
Note - The 1.5% Floating Loss Rule is an additional risk management rule and applies independently of the 3% Daily Drawdown and 6% Trailing Maximum Drawdown. All three rules must be respected at all times.
Exception: For all challenges purchased from September 2nd, 2026 onwards, the Floating Loss hard breach threshold is -1% instead of -1.5%. The example above scales accordingly — on a $50,000 account, this means the account is closed once combined open trade losses reach -$500 (-1%).

Minimum profitable day requirement for Instant Premium accountsCopy link
Traders on Instant Premium accounts must complete at least 5 profitable days before requesting a reward. These days do not have to be consecutive. To count as a minimum profitable day, the day must close with a profit of at least 0.5% of the initial balance, net of fees and commissions.
Consistency RuleCopy link
Instant Premium accounts do not have a consistency rule. There is no requirement for profits to be spread evenly across trading days. You are free to generate profits however they come, without needing to balance out a high-profit day.
