What rules can breach my account, and where do I find the limits?

Every account has two core loss limits, Daily Drawdown and Max Overall Drawdown, plus a few rule-based limits. Some rules also depend on whether you bought your account before or on/after 12 March 2026.

May 30, 2026
Copy for LLM

 

Your account can be breached in two ways: by crossing a loss limit (drawdown) or by breaking a trading rule. This page is the starting point. It explains each rule briefly and links to the article with the exact figures for your account type.

 

One important note:

Some rules changed on 12 March 2026. Which set applies to you depends on the date you purchased your account, not today's date. See the prohibited strategy section below.

 

 

The two loss limits that apply to every account

Daily Drawdown is the most you can lose in a single trading day. It resets at the start of each day (5:00 PM EST). How the daily floor is set depends on your plan: most plans fix it at a set percentage of your initial balance, measured from the higher of your balance or equity at the start of the day. The 3-Step instead trails it from your previous day's closing equity, and Instant Elite has no daily limit at all.

 

Max Overall Drawdown is the hard floor your account can never cross. On most plans, this is static: a fixed percentage below your initial balance that does not move when you make profit. On the 3-Step and both Instant plans it trails upward as your equity grows. The trailing version on the Instant plans stops rising once it reaches your initial balance, then locks there.

 

Static: fixed floor from initial balance. 

Trailing: floor follows equity. 

Trailing lock: trails up, then locks at initial balance.

Prohibited trading strategies (depends on your purchase date)

The drawdown limits above are the same regardless of when you bought your account. The list of prohibited strategies is not.

If you purchased your account before 12 March 2026, Reverse Hand Post Taking Losses, Martingale/Grid Trading, Lot Size Restriction, Position Stacking, All-In Trading, Excessive Scalping, and One-Sided Betting are prohibited on that account.

If you purchased your account on or after 12 March 2026, they are no longer prohibited.

 

This change applies per account, based on its purchase date, so an older account and a newer account registered to you can follow different rules. If you are unsure which set applies, check your purchase date or ask support with your account ID.

 

Other rules that can breach your account

These apply to some account types and not others. 

Lot size restriction for accounts purchased before March 12, 2026, applies to select account types.

1.5% risk per trade idea for funded accounts, except Prime.

Risk Limiter is a tighter limit the Risk Team can apply at its discretion.

 

What happens if you breach a rule

A breach does not always mean instant closure. Depending on what happened, the outcome ranges from a warning, to loss of payout for that cycle, to account termination for severe or repeated cases. See What Happens if You Breach the Rules.
