Trading Idea

 Trading Idea
 Risk per trading idea explained
 Updated today

 For Classic, Ultra and Rapid accounts:

 A trader must not risk more than 50% of the daily drawdown on a single trading idea. To allow the risk management team to calculate the risk accurately, a Stop Loss (SL) must be added to each trade within a maximum of two minutes of opening the position. For example:

 If a client wishes to open a BUY position on XAUUSD, risking 3% with 30 lots, this constitutes one trading idea. If those 30 lots are divided into three positions of 10 lots each, the risk per individual trade will be lower than 3%. However, the total risk associated with the trading idea will exceed 3%. Therefore, this practice will be considered gambling, as it breaches the 50% rule.

 For Instant Standard / Pro, Power, Flash & Prime accounts:

 A trader must not risk more than 1% on a single trading idea. To allow the risk management team to calculate the risk accurately, a Stop Loss (SL) must be added to each trade within a maximum of two minutes of opening the position.

 For Elite New accounts:

 A trader must not risk more than 2.50% on a single trading idea. To allow the risk management team to calculate the risk accurately, a Stop Loss (SL) must be added to each trade within a maximum of two minutes of opening the position.

 A trading idea refers to a group of trades that share a common rationale or objective and are executed on the same financial instrument. These trades may be opened simultaneously or within a few minutes of one another, provided they are in the same direction (BUY or SELL) and follow the same analysis or strategy used by the trader.

 Multiple trades will be considered part of the same trading idea if:

 They are opened on the same financial instrument.
 They are executed in the same direction (BUY or SELL).
 They are executed no more than 30 minutes apart.
 Once 30 minutes have passed since the last trade was closed, any new trades on the same instrument and in the same direction will be considered part of a new trading idea, unless there is a technical justification for treating them otherwise.

 Any trade at Breakeven (BE) or Take Profit (TP) will no longer contribute to the risk calculation. Therefore, the trader may continue taking risks on subsequent trades without the previous trade's risk being added.

 The trading idea and the risk taken will always be measured according to the highest risk defined by the Stop Loss. The initial Stop Loss will be the first valid reference for risk calculation. If subsequent modifications increase the risk, the highest Stop Loss risk recorded for the trade will be used.

 For example, if a trade has a Stop Loss representing 2% risk and closes with a profit, and the client subsequently opens another trade on the same instrument and in the same direction before at least 30 minutes have passed, that trade will be considered part of the same trading idea. The initial 2% Stop Loss risk will remain the reference for measuring risk.

 In other words, a trading idea may consist of one or several positions opened on the same instrument, with the objective of capitalising on a specific market opportunity identified by the trader through technical, fundamental or quantitative analysis.

 Instant Funding – Standard Model

 WSFUNDED's Standard Instant Funding Model provides immediate access to a funded account, with specific rules designed to manage trading risks and profits.

 Account Violations (Hard Breach)

 All instant funding models operate with a trailing drawdown.

 A hard breach occurs when a trader violates a fundamental risk management rule, such as exceeding the maximum daily loss or the maximum overall drawdown. These violations result in the immediate closure of the account.

 Maximum Daily Loss – 3%

 How is it calculated?
 The daily loss limit is calculated at 17:00 New York time (ET), at market close.

 If there are open positions, account equity is used, including floating profits or losses. If there are no open positions, the closed balance is used as the reference.

 The maximum permitted daily loss is equivalent to 3% of the initial account balance.

 Example:
 On a $50,000 account, the maximum permitted daily loss is $1,500. If the reference balance at the start of the day is $50,000, the account must not fall below $48,500 without breaching this rule.

 Maximum Overall Drawdown – 6% (Trailing)

 Instant Standard accounts use a trailing drawdown. This means that the maximum loss threshold increases as the account reaches new balance highs.

 The drawdown amount is equivalent to 6% of the initial account balance and remains fixed in monetary terms.

 As the trader generates profits and reaches new balance highs, the breach level also rises, following the highest balance achieved.

 If the balance falls to the established limit, the account may enter a hard breach.

 👉 Key point:

 The trailing drawdown only moves upwards when new balance highs are reached. It never moves downwards, even if losses occur afterwards.

 This requires consistent risk management, as profits also increase the minimum capital level that must be maintained.

 AFTER A WITHDRAWAL

 The breach level does not decrease or become locked at any point after a profit withdrawal.

 If a trader wishes to continue trading after a withdrawal, they must maintain a sufficient safety buffer between the remaining balance and the breach level.

 Otherwise, any loss or commission that brings the account balance to or below the breach level, depending on the account rules, may trigger a hard breach.

 Example 1 – Instant Standard (6% Trailing)

 A trader has a $100,000 account and reaches a maximum balance of $103,000, generating $3,000 in profit.

 The trader decides to withdraw the full $3,000 profit.

 Calculation:

 Initial balance: $100,000
 Highest balance reached: $103,000
 Permitted drawdown (6% of initial balance): $6,000
 Breach level: $97,000
 Withdrawal: $3,000
 Balance after withdrawal: $100,000
 Remaining buffer: $3,000

 After the withdrawal, the account retains a $3,000 buffer above the breach level. The trader may continue trading provided all risk limits are respected.

 Example 2 – Instant Standard (6% Trailing)

 A trader has a $100,000 account and reaches a maximum balance of $106,000, generating $6,000 in profit.

 The trader decides to withdraw the full $6,000 profit.

 Calculation:

 Initial balance: $100,000
 Highest balance reached: $106,000
 Permitted drawdown: $6,000
 Breach level: $100,000
 Withdrawal: $6,000
 Balance after withdrawal: $100,000
 Remaining buffer: $0

 After the withdrawal, the account balance is exactly at the breach level.

 The trader has no remaining buffer to absorb losses or commissions. Therefore, any negative movement that takes the balance below this level would trigger a hard breach.

 Example 3 – Instant Standard (6% Trailing)

 A trader has a $100,000 account and reaches a maximum balance of $107,000, generating $7,000 in profit.

 At this point, the breach level is $101,000.

 The trader continues trading and loses $3,000, reducing the account balance to $104,000.

 Although the balance has decreased, the breach level remains at $101,000 because the trailing drawdown never moves downwards.

 The trader has $4,000 in accumulated profit and must decide how much to withdraw.

 Balance after withdrawal: $101,000
