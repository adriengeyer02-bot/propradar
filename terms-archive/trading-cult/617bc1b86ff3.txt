Maximum Risk – 2% per Symbol and Direction This section consists of two separate controls: Max Risk per Trade and Max Open Loss per Symbol. Both rules are designed to measure and restrict exposure in different ways. A. Max Risk per Trade – 2% of Initial Balance * The maximum permitted risk on any individual symbol and direction must not exceed 2% of the account's Initial Balance. * Risk is calculated as the potential loss, in USD, if the trade reaches its Stop Loss, expressed as a percentage of the account's Initial Balance. Trading costs, such as commissions and swaps, are not included in this calculation. Multiple Positions on the Same Symbol * Where multiple positions in the same direction on the same symbol are open at the same time, their risk is combined when calculating the 2% limit. * Splitting a larger position into several smaller positions does not provide an additional risk allowance. For example, on an account with an Initial Balance of $100,000, the maximum permitted risk is $2,000 (2%) per symbol and direction. * If a trader has three EURUSD Buy positions open at the same time with potential losses at their respective Stop Loss levels of $500, $700 and $600, the combined EURUSD Buy risk is $1,800 (1.8%) and remains within the permitted limit. * Opening another EURUSD Buy position, or modifying an existing Stop Loss, so that the combined risk exceeds $2,000 (2%) would result in a breach. * Positions on different symbols are assessed separately. Hedged Positions * Where both Buy and Sell positions are open on the same symbol, the Buy and Sell sides are assessed separately for the Maximum Risk rule. * This means the combined risk of all open Buy positions on a symbol must remain within 2% of the Initial Balance, and the combined risk of all open Sell positions on the same symbol must also remain within 2%. * For example, on an account with an Initial Balance of $100,000, EURUSD Buy positions may have combined risk of up to $2,000 (2%), while EURUSD Sell positions may separately have combined risk of up to $2,000 (2%). When Is Risk Measured? * Risk is measured when a trade is opened and is also reassessed if the Stop Loss is subsequently modified. * Moving a Stop Loss further away from the entry price so that the individual or combined risk exceeds the applicable 2% limit will result in a breach. B. Max Open Loss per Symbol – 2% of Previous Day's End-of-Day Balance This rule limits the total floating loss a trader can have across all open positions on any single instrument. The combined floating loss of all open positions on the same symbol must not exceed 2% of the account's previous day's End-of-Day (EOD) balance. Unlike the Max Risk per Trade rule, this calculation is based on the previous day's EOD balance rather than the account's Initial Balance. Each symbol is assessed independently. Floating losses across different instruments are not combined. How Is the Open Loss Calculated? * The floating loss of all open positions on the same symbol is added together. * Both Buy and Sell positions on the same symbol are included when calculating the total floating loss for that symbol. * If the combined floating loss on any single symbol exceeds the permitted 2% limit, the account will be breached immediately. Example If the previous day's EOD balance was $10,000, the maximum permitted open loss per symbol is $200 (2%). - If a trader has three open EURUSD positions with a combined floating loss of $250, the permitted limit has been exceeded. - The account will therefore be breached immediately. - Losses on other instruments, such as GBPUSD or XAUUSD, are assessed separately and do not contribute to the EURUSD calculation. Daily Reset and Reference Balance * The previous day's EOD balance is used as the reference balance for this calculation. * The reference balance is updated at the daily reset, which takes place at 00:00 in the programme's trading timezone. * Following the reset, the new reference balance is used to calculate the permitted open loss for each symbol. 2. Minimum Trade Duration – 30 Seconds * Every trade must remain open for a minimum of 30 seconds. * Trade duration is measured from the time the position is opened until the time it is closed. * Any position that is closed less than 30 seconds after it was opened will result in a breach of this rule. 3. Trade Size Consistency – Maximum 5× Median * Trade sizes must remain reasonably consistent with the trader's previous trading activity. * Once an account has completed 10 trades, the Trade Size Consistency rule becomes applicable. * A new trade may not exceed 5 times (5×) the trader's median historical trade size. * Trade size is measured using USD exposure rather than lots. This allows trade sizes across instruments with different contract specifications to be measured consistently. What Is the Median Trade Size? * The median is the middle value of the trader's historical trade sizes when the trades are arranged from smallest to largest based on their USD exposure. * For example, if the trader's historical trade sizes are: $20,000 – $40,000 – $50,000 – $70,000 – $100,000, the median trade size is $50,000. * The maximum permitted size of a new trade would therefore be: $50,000 × 5 = $250,000 * A new trade exceeding $250,000 in USD exposure would breach the Trade Size Consistency rule. * Where there is an even number of historical trades, the median is calculated using the average of the two middle values. * The median is based on the trader's trading history and may therefore change as additional trades are completed. 4. Mandatory Stop Loss – 2 Minutes * Every trade must have a Stop Loss. * A Stop Loss can be placed when the trade is opened or added within a maximum of 2 minutes from the time the trade is opened. * If a position remains open for more than 2 minutes without a Stop Loss, the account will breach the Mandatory Stop Loss rule. Stop Loss and the 2% Maximum Risk Rule * Adding a Stop Loss within the permitted two-minute period does not override the Maximum Risk rule. * The Stop Loss must be positioned so that the potential loss remains within the applicable 2% Maximum Risk limit. * Moving an existing Stop Loss further away from the entry price is permitted only where the resulting risk remains within the applicable 2% limit. * If moving the Stop Loss causes the risk to exceed 2%, the account will breach the Maximum Risk rule. How is Max Total Loss calculated? The Max Total Loss limit varies depending on the type of challenge: * 2 Step Evaluation Challenges: The Max Total Loss is static and set as a fixed percentage of the Challenge Starting Balance. If profits are made on the account which increase the balance to match or exceed the initial Max Total Loss amount, the Max Total Loss limit locks in at the starting balance and stops adjusting. This means that, no matter how high the balance rises, the account will only breach the rule if it falls back to the original starting balance. If multiple payouts are made the Max Total Loss will only be recalculated if the account’s profit percentage relative to the initial starting balance exceeds the previous High Water Mark. If the profit percentage is lower than or equal to the previous High Water Mark, the Max Total Loss remains unchanged and continues to trail from the last locked High Water Mark. Once the Max Total Loss locks at the starting balance, it remains fixed, regardless of any future payouts or withdrawals.
