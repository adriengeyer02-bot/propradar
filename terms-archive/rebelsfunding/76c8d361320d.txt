BASIC INFORMATION
About RebelsFunding
Core information about the RebelsFunding business model, simulated accounts, eligibility and the difference between Evaluation and RCF cooperation.
For exact program parameters, always use the current Program Rules together with the Terms & Conditions.
01
What is RebelsFunding?
RebelsFunding is a prop trading education and evaluation service operated by RIFM, s.r.o. It provides structured simulated Evaluation programs for beginner and experienced traders who want to demonstrate trading ability, consistency and risk management on the RF-Trader platform.
After successful completion of an Evaluation, a trader may become eligible for a separate assessment and possible RCF cooperation. Successful Evaluation does not create an automatic right to an RCF Account, a payout or acceptance into cooperation.
Evaluation and RCF Accounts are fully simulated accounts using real market quotations. RebelsFunding is not a broker and program purchases are not deposits.

03
Is RebelsFunding a broker or liquidity provider?
No. RebelsFunding and RIFM, s.r.o. do not operate as a retail broker or liquidity provider for traders, do not accept trading deposits and do not execute customer investment orders on a live brokerage account.
RF-Trader uses real market quotations and technical services supplied through third-party infrastructure, while the Evaluation and RCF Accounts shown to traders remain fully simulated. Any proprietary trading activity performed by RIFM, FRCSM or an affiliated entity is independent, uses its own capital, is carried out on its own account and at its own risk.
04
Are RebelsFunding trading accounts real live accounts?
No. All Evaluation and RCF Accounts provided to traders are fully simulated accounts displaying real market quotations. The balances, trades, profit and loss shown on those accounts do not represent the trader trading deposited money or a live brokerage account.
Simulation has limitations. Execution, liquidity and results may differ from a live market environment, and there is no guarantee that a strategy or account will achieve a profit in simulated or live trading.
Important: A program purchase is a payment for the selected Evaluation service. It is not a trading deposit or an investment.
05
What is the difference between an Evaluation Account and an RCF Account?
Evaluation Account
A simulated account used to complete the selected paid program. It has program-specific Profit Targets, drawdown limits and a minimum number of real trades. Paid Evaluation trades must remain open for at least 30 seconds.
RCF Account
A fully simulated account available only after separate review, approval, compliance checks and an RCF Account Contract. It includes RCF-specific risk, news and minimum 60-second trade-duration rules and may generate an eligible RCF reward.
Completing an Evaluation makes the trader eligible for assessment. It does not guarantee that an RCF Account will be approved.

The trader must register and trade personally. Account sharing, trading for another person, allowing another person to trade the account and account-management services are prohibited.
Availability may change by country, payment method, sanctions status or compliance review. A successful Evaluation does not override these requirements.

06
What is a real trade in an RF Evaluation?
One real trade means one complete and independent trading idea based on a specific market movement or a new independent market setup. The count is not simply the number of positions opened.
Still one real trade
Several entries in the same direction during one market move.
One intended position divided into smaller parts.
A multi-step or multi-leg structure built around the same idea.
Trades on correlated instruments based on the same market view.
Adding to the same position without a new independent setup.
A new independent trade
The previous trading idea has been completed.
A new market movement or separate setup appears.
The reason for entry is genuinely different.
The new order is not merely a continuation or volume split of the previous idea.
Artificially dividing volume, opening complementary small positions or creating orders only to increase the trade count does not create additional real trades and may indicate a prohibited strategy.

GENERAL TRADING RULES
Trading Rules and Risk Limits
Practical answers about overnight and weekend holding, scalping, news, market close, instruments, drawdown, automated systems and prohibited practices.
Evaluation and RCF rules are not identical. Pay particular attention to the 30-second versus 60-second minimum duration, RCF risk limits and high-impact news restriction.
01
Can I hold trades overnight?
Yes. Overnight holding is allowed on Evaluation and RCF Accounts.
Use additional caution around daily rollover and periods of low liquidity. Spreads may widen, prices may gap and slippage may occur, especially on less liquid instruments. Protective orders do not guarantee execution at the requested price.
02
Can I hold trades over the weekend?
Yes. Existing positions may be held through the weekend on Evaluation and RCF Accounts.
Weekend holding carries additional gap and execution risk when the market reopens. You remain responsible for the effect on equity and all applicable Daily and Overall Drawdown limits.
03
Can I trade cryptocurrencies over the weekend?
Cryptocurrency positions may be held over the weekend, but opening new cryptocurrency trades is not available during the weekend on RF-Trader.
Existing positions remain subject to price movement, equity calculations and drawdown limits.
04
Can I open a trade shortly before the market closes?
No new trade or pending order may be opened or activated during the final 5 minutes before the relevant market closes.
An existing position may be closed or held through the closure. Check the current instrument schedule and holiday changes because different markets can close at different times.

10
Are Stop Loss and Take Profit mandatory?
Stop Loss and Take Profit are recommended but not generally mandatory under the standard paid Evaluation and RCF rules.
On an RCF Account, not using a Stop Loss does not remove the 1.5% maximum-risk rule. The complete trading idea is assessed using the highest floating or realized loss and other available data.
Execution warning: a Stop Loss is an instruction, not a guaranteed price. Gaps, low liquidity and slippage can result in execution at a different price.
11
What does Daily Drawdown mean?
Daily Drawdown is the maximum permitted intraday reduction in account equity measured from the daily starting-equity snapshot. The calculation includes closed results and current floating profit or loss.
The starting equity is recorded at the liquidity provider's daily rollover at 00:00 chart time. Chart time is UTC+3 during daylight saving time and UTC+2 during standard time, so the reset occurs at 21:00 UTC in summer and 22:00 UTC in winter. The trading server itself may display UTC.
Example: if starting equity is 10,000 USD and Daily Drawdown is 5%, the intraday equity floor is 9,500 USD.

13
What does Overall Drawdown mean?
Overall Drawdown is a fixed equity limit measured from the initial account size. It includes both closed results and floating profit or loss and does not move upward when the account becomes profitable.
Example: on a 10,000 USD account with a 10% Overall Drawdown, equity must never fall below 9,000 USD.

What is the maximum Overall Drawdown?
The fixed Overall Drawdown is 10% for Copper, Bronze, Silver and Free Trial accounts. It is 6% for Gold and Diamond accounts.
Competition accounts follow the separate Competition rules. The limit is always calculated using equity, including open profit or loss.
15
What happens after a Daily or Overall Drawdown breach?
Open positions are closed automatically.
The account is switched to read-only mode.
The affected Evaluation phase, Free Trial phase or RCF Account is terminated.
Trading cannot continue after the next Daily Drawdown reset.
No reward request may be submitted from a terminated RCF Account.
A positive balance does not create a right to a partial payout after the breach.
A drawdown breach is therefore a final account event, not a temporary daily pause.

01

03
Does RebelsFunding or FRCSM simply copy my RCF trades to a live market?
No. The RCF relationship should not be understood as automatic copying of the trader's simulated trades.

The execution or result of any real proprietary trade does not determine whether the trader earns an RCF reward or the amount of that reward.
04
What is the maximum risk allowed on an RCF Account?
The risk attributable to one trade or one group of related trades must not exceed 1.5% of the RCF Account balance recorded when the trade, or the first trade in the related group, was opened.
A related group can include split entries, gradual additions, positions on the same symbol, strongly correlated instruments or trades structured around the same market idea. Normal risk close to 0.5% per trade or related group is strongly recommended.
When no Stop Loss is used, the rule is assessed using the highest floating or realized loss and other available data relating to the complete trading idea.
Closing and reopening a position on the same instrument and in the same direction no more than 20 minutes after the previous position was closed may be assessed as a continuation of one trading idea, particularly where a position held for a longer period is briefly interrupted. This is relevant when counting real trades in Evaluation and when assessing the 1.5% risk limit on an RCF Account. The nature of the strategy and the substantive connection between entries are decisive, not the time interval alone. Independent trades based on new, separate market setups, including normal scalping, are not automatically grouped together. An interval longer than 20 minutes does not by itself guarantee separate assessment.
05
What is the minimum RCF trade duration and trade count?
Every trade opened on an RCF Account must remain open for at least 60 seconds.
There is no minimum number of trades required before an eligible RCF reward request. The account must still comply with all risk, news, prohibited-practice and payout conditions.
06
When can I request my first payout from an RCF Account?
The first eligible RCF reward request may be submitted not earlier than 14 calendar days after the first trade on the relevant RCF Account.
The period is calculated separately for each RCF Account. The account must remain active and compliant, and all open positions must be closed before the request is submitted.
This is the waiting period before a request, not the payment deadline. Under the RCF Account Contract, the approved Cash Portion is due within 14 calendar days from the later of issuance of the RCF Remuneration Approval Decision or receipt and acceptance of complete accounting, tax, KYC and payout documentation. A longer invoice payment period applies if accepted by RIFM.
07

All open positions on the relevant account must be closed before the request is submitted. A request cannot be made from a terminated RCF Account, including an account terminated by a Daily or Overall Drawdown breach.

Forex and Trading Basics
General educational explanations retained from the original FAQ for visitors who are new to Forex, leverage, swap, risk and execution.
This section explains general market concepts. It does not change the fact that RebelsFunding Evaluation and RCF Accounts are fully simulated and do not constitute investment advice.
01
What is Forex?
Forex, also known as the foreign exchange market, is the global market in which currencies are exchanged and priced against one another. It is decentralized and operates through a network of banks, brokers, institutions and other market participants.
Forex is one of the largest financial markets in the world, with trading activity measured in trillions of US dollars per day.
02
What is trading on Forex?
Currencies are quoted and traded in pairs, such as EUR/USD, GBP/JPY or AUD/USD. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency.
If EUR/USD is quoted at 1.20, one euro is valued at 1.20 US dollars. A trader may buy a pair when expecting the base currency to strengthen against the quote currency, or sell it when expecting the opposite.
On RebelsFunding accounts, this activity takes place inside a simulated environment using real market quotations.

Is there any risk with Forex trading?
Yes. Live Forex and financial-market trading carry a high degree of risk and may not be suitable for every person. Prices can move quickly because of economic data, interest-rate expectations, political events, liquidity and market sentiment.
Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. Before trading live capital, a person should understand market risk, execution risk and the possibility of losing the full amount committed to trading.
RebelsFunding accounts are simulated, but poor risk management can still breach the account rules and end an Evaluation or RCF Account.

What is trading leverage?
Leverage allows a trader to control a larger market exposure than the amount of margin required for the position. A leverage ratio of 1:100 means that 1 unit of margin can represent up to 100 units of market exposure.
Leverage is a double-edged tool. It increases the effect of favorable price movement, but it also increases the speed and size of losses.
On an RF simulated account, leverage defines the simulated exposure available for each instrument. It is not a loan or live capital deposited into the trader's name.

What is swap or rollover?
Swap or rollover is an overnight adjustment applied to a position that remains open across the instrument's rollover time. It can reflect interest-rate differences and other costs associated with the instrument.
The value may be positive, negative or marked as swap-free. Some instruments can also apply a larger adjustment on a specified triple-rollover day. Check the current RF-Trader specification before holding a position overnight.

Is trading on Forex or another financial market profitable?
Trading can be profitable for some traders, but there is no guaranteed strategy, account result or income. Every market approach can experience losses, drawdowns and periods in which previous assumptions stop working.
Long-term performance depends on market understanding, a tested strategy, execution discipline, position sizing and risk management. Simulated performance does not guarantee the same result in a live market.
07
What is slippage?
Slippage is the difference between the price requested or expected for an order and the price at which the order is actually executed. It can occur when the market moves before execution or when liquidity at the requested price is insufficient.
Positive slippage
The order is executed at a more favorable price than requested.
Negative slippage
The order is executed at a less favorable price than requested.
Slippage is more likely during news, low liquidity, rollover, market openings and price gaps. Stop Loss and other protective orders do not guarantee execution at the exact requested price.
