Trade with Freedom at FTM

None of the following reasons will ever become an issue in your trading journey at FTM.

No Toxic Trading Or Gambling Rule

There are no broad prohibitions on "gambling" or "toxic trading." Does this mean FTM promote this behavior? of course not. However, this is a term that carries a thousand different meanings. What one person considers gambling behavior, another considers a legitimate strategy. Because of that subjectivity and vagueness, there is no universal rule applied.

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

Imposing a restriction that is open to interpretation creates more problems than it solves. Traders lose clarity, trade with doubt, and never know for certain whether their next decision will be judged as acceptable or a violation.

The programs are designed so that inherently excessive gambling does not work in your favor. The more you try to force outcomes through high-risk behavior, the more likely you are to fail within the built-in limits. That is the safeguard, not a subjective behavioral rule. You are free to trade your own way, but the structure naturally rewards discipline and punishes recklessness.

No Lot Size Restrictions

Any lot size that your available margin and leverage permit is fully allowed. You will never be penalized for changing your position sizes from one trade to the next, regardless of how inconsistent that may appear.

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

Trading conditions change. Market volatility shifts, setups vary in conviction, and a trader's confidence naturally fluctuates. Forcing rigid consistency in lot sizes ignores the reality of discretionary trading. We trust you to manage your own risk within the parameters provided. If your margin allows the trade, the trade is valid. Period.

Martingale/Layering Allowed

These are common position-management approaches traders use:

Martingale: Increasing position size after a losing trade or adverse price movement in an attempt to recover previous losses when the market reverses.
Layering: Opening several smaller positions in the same direction at different price levels instead of one large entry.

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

Drawdown parameters exist for a reason. If you're confident you can manage your strategy within the allowed drawdown limits, we don't restrict you from using it. How you structure your trades on a single account is your call, as long as you stay within the rules.

This is allowed per account. However, running these strategies across multiple accounts simultaneously to create an unfair advantage is treated as abusive behavior and leads to reward deductions.

Learn more: Sequential Trading

No News Restrictions

There are no blackout windows, no time-based restrictions around economic announcements, and no profit reductions tied to news events. Every trade is treated equally regardless of when it was opened or closed. All profits from trades placed during (or previously held positions closed) news events are fully eligible for performance rewards.

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

Depending on your program type, you will be managing either a floating limit or a daily/overall loss limit. The safeguard is built into the structure itself, not into arbitrary time windows. Manage your risk parameters responsibly, stay within your limits, and trade the news freely.

No Margin Rules

Usually Margin rule caps the total margin usage across all open positions. The violation depends on exceeding a certain margin usage percentage.

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

We do not impose a margin usage rule. Leverage is set at a fixed, known level for a reason. If the available leverage allows you to open a trade at a specific lot size, you may do so. This will never be treated as a surprise violation when you request a performance reward.

No One-Sided Betting Rule

One-sided betting generally refers to repeatedly opening additional positions in the same direction (all buys or all sells) on the same symbol while an existing position is already open, or doing so in quick succession without a proper cooldown period. It's viewed as a form of directional gambling.




Opening another same-direction trade on a symbol while an existing trade is in profit and still open
Opening another same-direction trade on a symbol while an existing trade is in loss and still open
Cooldown Violation Opening a same-direction trade within a certain time of closing the previous one on that symbol

WHY THIS IS ALLOWED

Directional bias isn't a crime here. If you think a trade is going your way, you can add to it. If you think it's coming back, you can add there too. We get that doing this takes a plan, you still need to manage your trades properly. Asking traders to flat-out stop would take away a lot of freedom, and that's not something we want to do. So follow your read, keep your risk in check, and trade your plan.

Q.01
How Does the Maximum Daily Drawdown Limit for the 1-Step Infinity Evaluation Work?

The Maximum Daily Drawdown Limit for the 1-Step Infinity Evaluation is 3% of the Initial Balance.

Example 1:

For a $40,000 1-Step Infinity Evaluation, the Daily Drawdown is 3% of the Initial Balance.

Day 1:

Starting Balance/Equity: $40,000
Allowed Daily Drawdown: 3% of $40,000 = $1,200 (stop-out limit = $38,800)

Example 2:

Day 2:

End of Day Balance (Day 1): $44,000
End of Day Equity (Day 1): $43,000

At 5 PM ET, if unrealised positions are open, 3% of the Initial Balance will be deducted from the higher of the two values (Balance or Equity).

In Example 2 above, since Balance is higher than Equity, the stop-out limit will become:

$44,000 – (3% of $40,000) = $44,000 – $1,200 = $42,800

If either Equity or Balance reaches this limit, it will result in a breach of the Daily Drawdown Limit.

Example 3:

Day 3:

End of Day Balance (Day 2): $41,000
End of Day Equity (Day 2): $46,000

In Example 3 above, since Equity is higher than Balance, the stop-out limit will become:

$46,000 – (3% of $40,000) = $46,000 – $1,200 = $44,800

Since the Balance of $41,000 is lower than the stop-out limit of $44,800, this will cause an immediate breach of the Daily Drawdown Limit.

Breaching this limit will cause the account to be violated, closed, and disabled for trading.

Q.02
How Does the Overall Drawdown Limit for the 1-Step Infinity Account Work?

The Overall Drawdown Limit for the 1-Step Infinity Evaluation is 3% of the Initial Balance. This is a static drawdown limit, meaning the stop-out limit does not increase as the trader makes profits.

Example:

Account Size: $40,000
Overall Drawdown Limit: 3%

Day 1:

Starting Balance/Equity: $40,000
Overall Drawdown: $1,200
Stop-Out Limit: $38,800 ($40,000 – $1,200)

Day 2:

Starting Balance/Equity: $42,000
Overall Drawdown: $1,200
Stop-Out Limit: $38,800 ($40,000 – $1,200)

Even though the account has made a profit, the stop-out limit remains fixed at $38,800 throughout the evaluation and until the first approved performance reward on the funded account.

Please Note: After the first approved funded performance reward, the Overall Drawdown stop-out limit moves to the Initial Balance of the account. This adjustment occurs only once, and the limit remains fixed at the Initial Balance permanently, regardless of any future profits or performance reward.

If either Balance or Equity reaches the applicable Overall Drawdown stop-out limit, it will result in a breach.

Breaching this limit will cause the account to be violated, closed, and disabled for trading.

Q.05
Is there any buffer requirement on the 1-Step Infinity Program?

What is the buffer release?
Every time you reach 15 approved performance rewards on the same funded account, 30% of your total account buffer is unlocked and added to your payable balance. This is a reward for sustained, consistent performance — the longer you trade and request successfully, the more of the held buffer you get back.

How does the buffer work in the first place?
The buffer is a portion of your account balance held in reserve to protect against drawdown. It starts at 2% and increases by 2 percentage points every 3 approved performance rewards. It is not lost — it simply sits aside while you trade. The buffer release is how you eventually access it.

How is the released amount calculated?
At the point you hit your 15th approved performance reward, we calculate the total buffer held on your account at that time and release 30% of it to you as a payable reward.

Example — $40,000 account:
By performance reward 15, your buffer has stepped up to 10% of the account balance:
10% × $40,000 = $4,000 total buffer held
30% of that buffer is released:
30% × $4,000 = $1,200 released to you
That $1,200 is paid out at your current profit split rate and is subject to the same processing rules as a standard reward request.

Does the released amount count as one of my performance rewards?
No. The buffer release is a separate disbursement and does not count toward your performance reward ladder or advance your split tier.

Does this happen again after performance reward 15?
Yes. The same release applies at every 15-performance reward milestone on the same account — performance reward 15, performance reward 30, performance reward 45, and so on. The buffer amount at the point of each milestone is used to calculate the release at that time.

Does it reset if I get a performance reward rejected?
A rejected request does not count toward the 15-performance reward milestone, the same way it does not advance your split tier. Only approved performance rewards count.

Do I need to manage my own drawdown buffer on Infinity Plus?

Yes. While Infinity Plus removes the platform-enforced balance buffer, your account still operates under the standard 3% Overall Drawdown limit and since there is no system-imposed buffer holding balance back, you are responsible for managing your own safety margin.

On a $20,000 account for example, your Overall Drawdown stop-out is fixed at $19,400 ($20,000 minus 3%, which is $600). There is nothing preventing you from requesting your full available profit down to the 1% eligibility threshold, but if you do, you are trading with very little room before a breach.

Example — $20,000 account:

Initial Balance: $20,000

Stop-out limit: $19,400 (fixed, does not move until first approved reward)

Minimum balance to request: $20,200 (1% above initial)

If you request your full profit at $20,200, your remaining balance is $20,000 which is just $600 above stop-out

After your first approved reward, the Overall Drawdown stop-out moves permanently to $20,000 (your Initial Balance). From that point, any drawdown that brings your balance or equity to $20,000 results in a breach including from full payout requests.

There is no rule requiring you to leave a buffer but leaving one is strongly advisable. How much you keep as a personal safety margin is your decision to make as part of your risk management.

Q.07
What is the difference between 1-Step Infinity and Infinity Plus?

The 1-Step Infinity and Infinity Plus accounts share the same evaluation structure; a 9% profit target, a static 3% daily loss limit, a 3% maximum drawdown, no consistency rule, and no minimum trading days. Once funded, both allow you to request performance rewards any day you are eligible.




The differences are in how the funded stage operates:




Profit Split

With a standard Infinity account, your profit split starts at 80% and increases progressively, 85% on your fifth approved reward and 90% from your sixth onwards. With Infinity Plus, your split is fixed at 80% on every approved reward and does not increase over time.




Performance Reward Eligibility

To request a performance reward on an Infinity Plus account, your balance must be at least 1% above your initial account balance. On a $20,000 account, that means your balance must reach $20,200 before you are eligible. There is no buffer requirement and no requirement to generate additional profit between consecutive requests, as long as you are 1% above your initial balance, you may request.




Performance Reward Cap

There is no maximum limit on how much you can request per reward. You may request any amount from the $50 minimum up to your full available balance above the 1% threshold.




Account Size and Allocation

Infinity Plus is available up to $20,000. The maximum combined allocation across Infinity and Infinity Plus accounts is $60,000.




Which should I choose?

Infinity Plus suits traders who want straightforward, unrestricted access to their profits with no buffer management. Standard Infinity suits traders who want access to larger account sizes and are willing to work through a structured eligibility system in exchange for a rising split that reaches 90%.

How Drawdown Types Work?

Drawdown is the loss limit that ends an account. Every program carries two: a daily limit that resets each day, and an overall limit that runs for the life of the account. Here is exactly how each one is measured.

DAILY DRAWDOWN
Balance Based

A fresh stop-out each day, based on your balance.

Each day the stop-out is set from the greater of your balance or equity at the start of the day, minus a fixed dollar amount equal to your daily DD% of the Initial Balance. Only the daily starting point moves; the dollar amount never changes. An intraday dip can breach it even if you close the day in profit.

OVERALL DRAWDOWN
Static

Stop-out fixed to the Initial Balance.

The stop-out stays anchored to your starting balance for the life of the account. On a $100,000 account with a 10% limit it sits at $90,000, even after profits. Gains never raise the floor; only the Initial Balance defines it.

Trailing

Stop-out trails the Highest Recorded Balance.

The stop-out follows your highest recorded balance, holding the same dollar buffer below each new peak. It only ever moves up, never back down, so a pullback after a strong run can still breach it.

Trailing Lock

Trails, then fixes to the Initial Balance after +DD% profit.

It behaves like trailing until your balance reaches the Initial Balance plus the DD%. At that milestone the limit fixes to the Initial Balance for good, so later pullbacks respect that floor and the profit you already earned stays protected.
