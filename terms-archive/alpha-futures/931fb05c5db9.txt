 This guide breaks down futures prop firm rules — including Maximum Loss Limit (MLL), consistency, daily loss limits, and performance-fee eligibility — so you know what matters before you buy an evaluation.Futures prop firm rules are the Trading Objectives on a simulated evaluation and Qualified Account: profit targets, Maximum Loss Limit (MLL), daily loss limits, consistency (where applicable), news restrictions, position limits, and prohibited strategies. They are not optional tips. Breach the published rules and you fail the attempt or lose eligibility. This page explains what each rule type means and where Alpha Futures applies them.General information only, not financial advice. Simulated trading. Performance fees are performance-based; outcomes not guaranteed.
Why prop firm rules existFirms run simulated futures programmes so traders can prove risk control before Qualified Account access. Rules encode:Risk caps (how much you can lose)Objective targets (what “pass” means)Behaviour limits (news, hedging, position size)Performance fee eligibility (separate from passing the evaluation)Rules vary by plan and stage (evaluation vs Qualified). What is futures prop firm MLL?Futures prop firm MLL (Maximum Loss Limit) is the drawdown floor on a simulated evaluation or Qualified Account. If equity breaches the MLL, the account closes. Alpha Futures uses end-of-day trailing MLL on its plans; dollar amounts and percentages vary by plan and size. See also our MLL explained guide.
