For the purposes of these Terms, a Non-Executable Fill (“Non-Executable Fill”) is a simulated fill that could not have been executed under live market conditions at the time the instrument was traded in the simulated environment. A simulated fill is a Non-Executable Fill where Independent Market Data for the same instrument shows that no transaction occurred at or through the fill price during the Applicable Measurement Period (the “Applicable Measurement Period”), subject to a timing tolerance of fifty (50) milliseconds to account for timestamp alignment between ETF's simulated environment and the Independent Market Data source.

For the purposes of the Terms, Independent Market Data (“Independent Market Data”) means the official transaction record for the relevant instrument, as reported by the exchange of execution and sourced through our third-party market data provider, as ETF may designate.

The Applicable Measurement Period is:

(a) for an order resting in the market prior to the fill, the full interval during which the order was resting at the fill price or better; or

(b) for an order filled at or near the moment of placement, the instant the simulated trade was filled, adjusted by the fifty (50) millisecond tolerance period noted in the preceding paragraphs.

Where ETF identifies one or more Non-Executable Fills in an Account, ETF will reverse any gains attributable to those fills, on the basis that such gains did not arise from a trade that would have been possible in live market conditions.

Genuine Activity

If a Funded Account shows no Genuine Activity, ETF reserves the right to close that Account without notice. When an Account shows a prolonged pattern of scratch days or minimal trading progress, it may be reviewed for closure.  

Genuine Activity means your activity looks like real trading. You place trades based on your read of the market, manage risk, and follow whatever process you normally follow to make profit in your Account. A Genuine Activity trade fits the way you trade when you are trying to profit in your Account. 

Automated trading that is not expressly authorized in writing by ETF under Section (ac) does not count as Genuine Activity. Trades placed by a bot, script, or any automated system that is not so authorized are not allowed and do not qualify as Genuine Activity, even when the orders look real. 

A “scratch trade” is a trade placed just to log a trade, with no real intent behind it and is not Genuine Activity. A trader opens a position and closes it shortly thereafter at breakeven for no reason other than to log trading activity. 

On its own, a scratch trade is fine. Breakeven exits are part of real trading. The problem is when scratch trades become a pattern. 

How we review this activity

There are two situations related to non-Genuine Activity that can lead to closure, and they are reviewed differently. 

The first is a pattern of non-productive activity over time. This is not based on isolated trading days. We look at the full activity history of the account to decide whether it reflects consistent and legitimate participation. Occasional scratch trades are not an issue. The concern is when non-productive sessions become a pattern over an extended period, with little sign of genuine progress. If your usual behavior is placing scratch trades, that does not count as Genuine Activity, even when it is consistent. 

The second is a trade placed with no genuine intent other than to satisfy the weekly trading requirement. A trader can trade genuinely for weeks or months, then switch to scratch trades to keep an Account open.

A trading break may be requested once per quarter. Accounts have real, ongoing costs to maintain. Failure to execute a trade for a week, without an approved break, will result in Account closure and void any otherwise eligible Rewards. This rule applies to all Account types. 

The weekly trade has to be a Genuine Activity trade with the goal of profitability. A scratch trade placed only to reset the weekly clock does not satisfy the weekly trading requirement. If you cannot trade in a given week, reach out to us through the trading break form above. We would much rather you take a break than fake activity to keep an Account open.

Staying in good standing: Use your Account with genuine intent and keep a pattern of meaningful engagement. Steady, legitimate participation over time is what keeps an Account in good standing, not extended stretches of minimal or non-productive activity.
